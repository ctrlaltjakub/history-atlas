AN INDEPENDENT PODCAST CATALOGUE

About the project

A way to browse The Rest Is History by historical period and place. The podcast is hosted by Tom Holland and Dominic Sandbrook and produced by Goalhanger. This is an unofficial project.

Series first

Episodes released together as a series appear as one tile throughout the timeline, map, index and topic lists. All series share a burgundy accent from the project’s palette. Open a series to see its episodes in release order, then choose a part for its description, listening links and individual placement. Series memberships are based on the official archive and checked against the public feed. Standalone episodes appear individually.

The timeline

Every tile fills one column and one row, with the same size and padding. Its centre is snapped to the century or decade containing the middle date of its reviewed historical range. Tile size does not represent duration. A series keeps the range of all its dated parts when filtering. On desktop, hover or use the keyboard to focus a tile: a guide above the century and decade labels marks its approximate start and end years. Arrows indicate endpoints beyond the visible area. On mobile, open the tile for its dates and placement notes.

Before 1700, columns show centuries; from the 1700s onward, they show decades. The opening view shows the 16th and 17th centuries. Intermediate periods without tile centres or range endpoints may be compressed, marked by an ellipsis (⋯), so the whole timeline is not a uniform time scale. A decade covers ten calendar years, such as 1700–1709. Earlier centuries follow conventional numbering, with the final century column ending at 1699; there is no year zero.

Each column fills from the top, without empty rows between tiles. The busiest column sets the fixed number of rows for the whole table. Scrolling does not rearrange the tiles or change the table’s height. Scroll sideways through time and vertically for additional rows. Search, era and region filters match individual episodes, then show their series once. Era filters include any episode whose date range overlaps that era.

A release collection does not necessarily have a shared historical period. The 32-country World Cup collection, national anthems, comparisons across eras and broad country surveys appear under Episodes not on a timeline. Individual parts keep any useful reviewed dates in their details. These exclusions are editorial decisions recorded with reasons in the public repository; they do not remove episodes from the map or index.

The map

The map uses the Equal Earth projection and approximate subject locations. A series appears once, at its first mappable episode in release order; its parts may cover other places. From an episode’s details, Show on the map highlights that episode’s own location. Zoom to separate clusters, or select a shared location to browse its series and standalone episodes.

Dates and locations are independent: an episode may appear on the map without a timeline date, or on the timeline without a map location. Off-timeline parts also appear under Episodes not on a timeline, grouped by series and topic. The Worldwide group contains items without a single geographic setting. Modern coastlines and borders are for orientation.

The catalogue & listening links

Pocket Casts opens the matching episode. Spotify and Apple Podcasts open a verified episode where available; search fallbacks are labelled. YouTube links are searches. Original public audio is playable here. Location shortcuts reveal an episode in the full catalogue and clear filters that could hide it.
