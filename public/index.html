SOURCES & METHODOLOGY

About this atlas

This is an independent, unofficial companion to The Rest Is History, hosted by Tom Holland and Dominic Sandbrook and produced by Goalhanger.

How the timeline works

Episodes run from earlier to later by the beginning of the historical period they cover. Related episodes share a row. Series that overlap on the timeline occupy separate rows; rows are reused when space is available. The full set of rows stays in place while you scroll. The table height is set by the maximum row count for the selected episodes. Dates and ranges are editorial approximations, not timestamps or claims that a whole episode concerns a single year. Open an episode for its period and placement note.

Gaps between dates are compressed; distance does not represent a fixed number of years. The era buttons select episodes by the beginning of their period. Open broad surveys to see their full date range.

The region filter uses the place most central to each episode. International subjects are tagged “Across the world”. Myths, questions and discussions without a meaningful historical date appear under “Other episodes”.

How the map works

The map uses the Equal Earth projection. Each episode has one approximate subject location, with a place and an explanation in its details. Broad surveys use a representative location; myths use their traditional setting. Discussions without a meaningful geographic anchor are in the Worldwide group. Nearby episodes cluster together. Zoom in to separate places, then select a cluster to browse episodes at the same location. Modern coastlines and borders are for orientation, not historical boundaries. The map follows the search, era and region filters above.

The collection & listening links

Pocket Casts buttons open the matching episode. Spotify opens the matching episode when verified, or an explicitly labelled search. Apple Podcasts opens the episode when an exact match is available; otherwise its button says “Find episode”. YouTube links are explicitly labelled searches. The original public audio is also playable here.
